# Esquinazo — design guidelines

Persistent design context for esquinazo.io. Read this before any design work.

## Design Context

### Users

Football fans who came to check a score and leave. Not analysts, not fantasy managers — people
who want to know whether their side scored, where they sit in the table, and when the next match
kicks off. Technical level is irrelevant to them: this is not a tool they learn, it is a page
they glance at.

Usage splits genuinely between phone and desktop, so neither is a compromise of the other. Phone
use is often mid-activity — walking, in a pub, second screen during a match — which means content
must survive being read at arm's length, in bad light, one-handed. Desktop use is browsing:
tables, squads, a longer sit.

The emotional goal is **partisan relief or partisan misery, delivered fast**. Nobody visits to
feel productive. They visit to find out, and to feel something about what they find.

### Brand Personality

**Terrace. Raw. Partisan.**

Matchday programme, not broadcast package. Fanzine energy — heavier ink, rougher edges, attitude
in the voice. It sounds like someone who goes to games, not someone reading an autocue. It has
opinions about football even though it never has opinions about *your* club.

Crucially it is **not** neutral-corporate, not a sportsbook, and not a dashboard. It should feel
made by a person.

### Aesthetic Direction

Dark only, no theme toggle. Near-black ground, burnt orange as the single dominant accent, deep
red as its darker partner.

| Token | Value | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ink` | `#0a0a0a` | Page ground |
| `ink-raised` | `#141414` | Cards, hover surfaces |
| `ink-line` | `#232120` | Hairlines (warmed toward the accent) |
| `ink-bright` | `#f5f5f5` | Primary text |
| `ink-muted` | `#a0a0a0` | Secondary text |
| `ember` | `#cc5500` | Primary accent |
| `ember-bright` | `#ff6f14` | Accent for small text on raised surfaces (contrast) |
| `blood` | `#8b0000` | Losses, relegation, away side |

Type: **Archivo** (variable width axis, run expanded) for display; **Manrope** for body; tabular
figures (`.tnum`) everywhere numbers sit in a column.

**References:** matchday programmes, fanzines, terrace print, stadium signage, ticket stubs.
**Anti-references:** sportsbook/betting UI, neutral broadcast graphics, analytics dashboards,
generic dark-mode SaaS, anything that looks like it has a sales team.

**Constraints that shape design, not just engineering:**

- The data provider's free tier carries **no goal minutes, no lineups, no news feed**, and serves
  players **by squad, not globally**. Never design an interface that implies data we cannot fetch —
  a timeline with fake minutes is worse than a smaller, honest one.
- Responses are cached hard (60s fixtures, 5m tables, 60m squads). Design for content that is
  *nearly* live, and say so rather than implying real-time.
- No ads, no accounts, no tracking, no interstitials. Funded by donations. The absence of those
  patterns is part of the brand and should be visible, not hidden.

### Design Principles

1. **Score first, texture second.** The fastest path to a scoreline is never traded for
   atmosphere. Rawness lives in typography, surface and voice — never in extra taps, extra
   chrome, or density that slows a glance. If a decorative choice adds a step, it loses.

2. **Partisan voice, neutral numbers.** Copy can have attitude, edge and humour. Data never
   editorialises: no club gets favourable sorting, colouring or emphasis. The tone is a fan's;
   the table is a referee's.

3. **Earn the ink.** Heavy marks are welcome — thick rules, big type, saturated colour — but every
   heavy element must be load-bearing. Weight signals importance, so weight applied to everything
   signals nothing. One glow, spent on live content.

4. **Both ends, no amputation.** Phone and desktop are both primary. Adapt layouts rather than
   hiding function: filters become a horizontal scroller on mobile, not a buried menu. Nothing a
   desktop visitor can do should be unreachable on a phone.

5. **Never let colour carry meaning alone.** WCAG 2.1 AA is the floor — 4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large
   text and UI. Form badges carry W/D/L letters as well as colour; live states carry a label as
   well as a pulse.

## Current state vs. this direction

The frontend as built runs **broadcast/precise/unsentimental** — restrained hairlines, neutral
reporting voice, one deliberate glow. The direction above is **terrace/raw/partisan**. Bringing it
in line means heavier ink and rougher edges in the display layer, and real attitude in the copy,
*without* violating principle 1.

Known open item: `ember` (`#cc5500`) on `ink-raised` (`#141414`) measures **4.27:1** — under AA for
small text. Use `ember-bright` for small text on raised surfaces.
